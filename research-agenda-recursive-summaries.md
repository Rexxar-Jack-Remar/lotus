
# Research Agenda: Certified Separability for Two-Sided Recursive Program Analysis

## Thesis

Two-sided recursion is difficult not because recursive context appears on both sides, but because the left and right contexts may remain correlated.

We propose a theory of **certified separability**: represent the paired left–right contexts generated by a recursive summary system as a finite combination of independent components, solve those components directly in the original analysis algebra, and use tensor-product solving only when the correlation cannot be eliminated.

## 1. Problem

Newtonian program analysis produces linear recursive systems

[
X_i
===

c_i
\oplus
\bigoplus_{(a,j,b)\in T_i}
aX_jb.
]

The tensor-product construction solves such systems by retaining the left and right contexts separately in a paired domain. This is general, but potentially more expensive than ordinary algebraic elimination in the original domain (K).

The goal is not to characterize every system with a rational solution. It is to identify a principled class for which the paired information has a finite, checkable separable representation.

## 2. Motivating observation

Mixed recursion does not necessarily create synchronization.

For

[
X=c\oplus aX\oplus Xb,
]

the least solution is

[
X=a^*cb^*,
]

even when (a) and (b) do not commute.

The generated left and right contexts vary independently:

[
{(a^m,b^n)\mid m,n\geq 0}
=========================

{a^m\mid m\geq0}
\times
{b^n\mid n\geq0}.
]

By contrast,

[
X=c\oplus aXb
]

generates

[
{(a^n,b^n)\mid n\geq0},
]

which preserves a synchronization constraint between the two sides.

The central distinction is therefore:

> **independent two-sided context versus irreducibly correlated two-sided context.**

## 3. Paired-context semantics

Associate each recursive derivation with a pair

[
(u,v),
]

where (u) accumulates on the left of a base contribution and (v) accumulates on the right.

For each unknown (X_i), let (\mathcal C_i) denote its set, relation, or weighted representation of reachable context pairs.

Tensor-product analysis retains (\mathcal C_i) directly. Certified separability instead seeks a finite decomposition

[
\mathcal C_i
============

\bigcup_{k=1}^{r}
L_{ik}\times R_{ik},
]

or, in the algebraic setting,

[
S_i
===

\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}
\ell_{ik}\otimes r_{ik}.
]

Each component is separable: its left and right behavior can be computed independently.

The least summary can then be read out in the original domain as a finite expression of the form

[
X_i
===

\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}
\ell_{ik},c_{ik},r_{ik}.
]

This motivates a semantic measure of left–right correlation: the minimum number of separable components required to represent the paired-context solution.

## 4. Main theoretical agenda

### Theorem A — Finite separability implies direct solvability

If the paired-context solution of a recursive system admits a finite regular separable decomposition, then its least solution can be computed using finitely many regular operations in the original algebra (K).

This theorem should state the weakest algebraic assumptions required for:

* regular left and right closure;
* finite joins;
* least-fixed-point reasoning; and
* sound readout from paired contexts.

### Theorem B — Certificates denote separable decompositions

Define a small certificate calculus whose accepted derivations construct finite separable representations.

The initial certificate forms should include:

* one-sided elimination;
* independent prefix/suffix closure;
* block decomposition;
* acyclic substitution; and
* algebraically justified centralization.

Prove that every accepted certificate denotes a valid finite separable decomposition and therefore preserves the least solution.

This makes the rules instances of one semantic principle rather than a list of unrelated optimizations.

### Theorem C — Complete synthesis for a natural tractable class

Define an independently motivated structural class of systems whose paired-context automata have bounded product structure—for example, bounded-width product decompositions or a restricted class of finite unions of regular rectangles.

Give a polynomial-time algorithm that synthesizes certificates for every system in this class.

The completeness claim should be relative to this structural class, not to a fragment defined by the synthesis algorithm itself.

### Theorem D — Synchronization lower bound

In a free noncommutative model, prove that synchronized families such as

[
{(a^n,b^n)\mid n\geq0}
]

do not admit finite rectangular decompositions.

More generally, construct recursive system families whose separability rank is unbounded.

This theorem provides a principled explanation of why tensorization is sometimes necessary: it preserves correlations that no bounded separable representation can express.

## 5. Algebra-specific rank reduction

Some systems may be inseparable in the free syntactic model but separable in a particular analysis algebra.

For example, if a coefficient (b) is certified to commute with every value contributing to (X_j), then

[
aX_jb=abX_j.
]

Such an equality moves a paired action to one side and can reduce the separability rank of the system.

The certificate must contain checkable evidence, not merely an untrusted `commutes` answer. Possible witnesses include:

* disjoint state support;
* equality in a finite algebra;
* symbolic transformer identities; or
* derivations from previously certified algebraic laws.

This creates a clean separation between:

* **structural separability**, visible from the equation system; and
* **semantic separability**, exposed by equations valid in a particular abstract domain.

## 6. Algorithmic architecture

For each linearized Newton system:

1. decompose the dependency graph into SCCs;
2. solve acyclic and one-sided components directly;
3. construct a paired-context representation for each remaining SCC;
4. search for a finite separable decomposition;
5. apply certified algebraic rewrites that may reduce correlation;
6. check the resulting certificate independently;
7. solve certified components in the original domain; and
8. send residual components to the tensor-product solver.

The synthesis procedure may be heuristic. Correctness depends only on the small certificate checker.

Different SCCs—and potentially different blocks of one transformed SCC—may use direct or tensor solving.

## 7. Relationship to tensorization

The proposed theory should reinterpret tensorization rather than compete with it.

* Tensorization represents arbitrary left–right correlation.
* Direct one-sided solving represents degenerate separability.
* Prefix/suffix closure represents rank-one separability.
* Block decomposition represents finite separability.
* Centralization can collapse rank using domain equations.
* Synchronized recursion witnesses the need for unrestricted paired representations.

This gives a single conceptual spectrum rather than a binary choice between direct and tensor solving.

## 8. Evaluation

The empirical study should answer four questions.

### Prevalence

How many recursive SCCs have:

* rank-one separability;
* small finite separability;
* separability only after algebra-specific rewrites; or
* residual synchronized behavior?

### Benefit

How much tensor-domain work, memory, and running time is avoided?

Compare:

1. tensor solving only;
2. one-sided optimization;
3. structural separability;
4. structural plus algebra-specific certificates.

### Nontriviality

Do realistic examples require more than scalar prefix/suffix closure?

The evaluation should contain:

* mutually recursive finite-rank examples;
* certificates that use domain-specific equalities; and
* residual cases that genuinely require tensorization.

### Overhead

When does certificate synthesis cost more than it saves?

Measure synthesis, checking, direct solving, and fallback costs separately.

At least two genuinely noncommutative analysis domains should be included.

## 9. Project milestones

### Milestone 1: semantic foundation

* Formalize paired-context semantics.
* Prove finite separability implies direct solvability.
* Prove the synchronized diagonal has no finite rectangular decomposition.

### Milestone 2: tractable structural class

* Define a natural bounded-separability class.
* Give certificate synthesis and checking algorithms.
* Prove polynomial-time completeness for that class.

### Milestone 3: algebra-specific reduction

* Formalize certified centralization.
* Identify realistic noncommutative domains with useful proof objects.
* Construct examples where domain equations strictly reduce separability rank.

### Milestone 4: hybrid NPA solver

* Integrate direct and tensor solving.
* Prove preservation of every Newton subproblem and iterate.
* Evaluate prevalence, performance, and synthesis overhead.

## 10. Go/no-go criteria

A strong POPL submission should satisfy all four conditions:

1. finite separability is formalized as a genuine semantic invariant;
2. synchronized recursion has a nontrivial lower bound in the same framework;
3. certificate synthesis is complete for a natural, nontrivial structural class; and
4. realistic noncommutative analyses exhibit small separability or algebra-induced rank reduction.

If only the sound checker and performance optimization succeed, the result remains useful but is better framed as a solver paper.

## Recommended paper claim

> We show that the difficulty of two-sided recursive program summaries arises from correlation between their left and right contexts. We formalize this correlation through finite separability, prove that finitely separable systems can be solved directly in the original analysis algebra, and establish lower bounds for synchronized recursion. We give polynomial-time synthesis of checkable certificates for a natural bounded-separability class, extend the method with domain-specific algebraic witnesses, and integrate it with tensor-product Newtonian program analysis as a semantics-preserving hybrid solver.

This version makes the local identity (a^*cb^*), the certificate architecture, and the tensor fallback consequences of one central distinction: **finite separability versus persistent synchronization**.
